The fix makes the profile hold the kept gains after every closed window; it changes nothing the tuner decides. Built only together with the early closing (the Pico W's image, 936.9 KB of flash against 934.8 KB, static RAM unchanged), never on its own, never run on a unit.

**Not this ADR's: the early closing** (`ba847b9`): `charge_tuner.c` and `step2_tuner.py` closing a window from 8 throws once its difference is twice its noise; a trial kept early thrown on until its window is full; `tests/step2_sim.py` checking that the C and the Python close each window at the same throw; the study, `tools/charge_sim/step2_early_study.py`. ## Gate 1: the throw record

**Built 2026-11-28 (`94d13d5`), run on the Pico W the same evening.** No line of the auto-tuner, its twin, or how the charge drives it was changed.

What was built:
- `src/services/charge/charge_throws.c`: the newest 32 records and the one open, 2,912 bytes (88 a record) taken from the FreeRTOS heap at the first charge and kept, as the controller is; none to have, the charge ends at once, "No memory". The charge's task alone writes; a reader copies a whole record under a critical section. Static RAM: one pointer.
- `charge_mode.c` fills it: opened as a throw begins (its number, load, powder, target, whether the scale is simulated: the console's or the stored driver); its gains, thresholds and switches as the pour is configured, `by` the auto-tuner's trial when the gains thrown differ from the profile's with the tuner on; at the coarse tube's stop its time, the reading and the speed asked just before; at the fine stop the same and the fine tube's part; the pauses; closed at the verdict before `throw finished`, or as the throw ends short before `throw ended`, the reading at its end as its weight.

## Gate 3: the PIN

**Built 2026-11-29 as the owner meant it** (`09ea236c` and after), run on the Pico W the same night.

**A first build missed what the PIN is for** (2026-11-28, `9b0d41b` to `5002ee21`): it followed the gate's text then, a PIN a person chose, stored in the Wi-Fi record, set and cleared on the web (`POST /api/v1/pin`, Settings › PIN), cleared at the unit by a Clear PIN row or by a factory reset. It passed its checks, a factory reset with the owner at the unit among them. The owner then: "The PIN is never designed a one off thing. I want the PIN to be always visible on the unit, by entering the WiFi page ... it can be generated on startup ... the PIN will be held true with cookie, or the PIN is updated, i.e,. the unit rebooted. Do you see the gap now?" The gap: the PIN is a check that whoever changes the unit can read its screen, not a password a person keeps. The README's gate 3 was rewritten with his answers (a switch off by default, under the Wi-Fi page and set at the unit; none on the unit's own hotspot; 4 digits) and the stored PIN, its action, its Settings part and Clear PIN were taken out. What stayed: the server's cookie read and its write guard, the lockout, the index's `pin`, the portal's prompt, the guide's step 0.

What is built:
- **The switch:** `settings/wifi` `pin_required`, off by default, applying at once. Stored as a word appended at 108, past the release's tail padding (a byte would lie in it); its low byte is the boolean the settings read. The Wi-Fi record is 112 bytes; a record from before reads it as off. On the unit: the Wi-Fi page's button is now "Options" (a notes page has one button; the owner chose it over making the page a list), opening `wifi/options`: Wi-Fi On/Off (what "Turn Wi-Fi off" did) and Web PIN On/Off, each stored at once, as a toggle is. On the web: a field of the Wi-Fi form, from the schema.
- **The PIN:** 4 digits from `chip_random32()` at the Wi-Fi's start, in RAM alone (5 bytes), shown on the Wi-Fi page as "Web PIN" while the switch is on.
- **The check:** the server reads the cookie's `pin=` and, before any write to `/api/`, asks the API's guard, giving it the unit's address the client reached (`lwip_getsockname`, read only then: nothing kept). - **The guide's step 0:** the index's `pin`, ask the person to read the PIN off the Wi-Fi page, the cookie, the refusals.
- **Tools:** `pin_check` (below); `--pin`; the stand-in asks for `1234` while its Wi-Fi's switch is on.
