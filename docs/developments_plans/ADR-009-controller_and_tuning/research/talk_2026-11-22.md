## The owner's proposal, as he wrote it

1. "Waiting for scale to reach steady state is not a good strategy, as some scale has slow reporting interval and longer measurement lag, making the trickling period exponentially longer than A&D, and the current A&D speed with the new algorithm is not acceptable."
2. "Can we combine both, i.e., running prediction in parallel with stream charge, and signal the early stop if the trickling rate is potentially exceeding the charge limit."
3. "If under charge, the controller should continue to top up at minimum speed, with prediction until reaching +stop threshold."
4. "Can we integrate the continuous learning for a session, i.e., no change of powder or quitting the current session etc. into the pid system?"

His verdict, as he gave it: "the new controller is a not a replacement of the PID. Instead I think we should explore the option to run two in parallel, and learn, and signal the early stop if the PID got wrong for a specific trickle?"

## The agent's answers, as given

- **On waiting (1):** half right by these runs.
  - **What ending the wait saves:** ending the rest when the forecast holds nothing more saves 1.5 to 2.5 s and halves the long tail (the 95th percentile 26 to 20 s with AR2209, 73 to 36 s on the slow scale), but costs 6 to 10 points, because powder still lands after the forecast says the air is empty.
  - **Where the time goes on a slow scale:** most of it is not the wait but the approach. For a stop to be accurate, what is in the air at the stop must scatter less than the band; what is in the air is the flow times the scale's lag. So the slower the scale, the slower the flow at the stop must be, and the finish's time grows with the lag: in proportion, not exponentially.
  - **Speed on a slow scale:** a loop that is quick on a slow scale is over (the PID loop: 100%). Speed there has to come from a stop whose powder in the air is better known, not from waiting less.
  - **On the A&D:** the time worth taking is the tail. Series D's throws with no small move took a median 10.7 s; those with moves 12 to 34 s, each move's wait 2 to 4.4 s.
- **On the forecast in parallel with the PID stream (2):**
  - **What it does:** it takes a third of the PID loop's overs away, in less time than the staged controller.
  - **Why it stops there:** the PID loop slows down on the reading, which lags, so the fine tube is still fast when the forecast stops it, and the powder after a fast stop scatters widely. The forecast has to set the speed, not only the stop.
  - **The forecast with the owner's gains:** better with AR2209 and worse with BM2, because those gains were found by hand on the lagging reading. On the forecast the calibrated gain is about 2.2 rev/s a grain against 0.8. A PID loop on the forecast with calibrated gains and a floor set from the scale's lag is what the glide already is.
- **On topping up at the least speed with the forecast (3):** agreed as the way to finish (the glide's creep is that), with two conditions from the runs.
  - **The creep speed:** the least speed must follow the scale and the powder (here 0.029 rev/s, from the band over the lag), not the profile's 0.08, at which a stop's powder scatters by two to three digits.
  - **One settled reading:** a throw at 0.02 gr still needs one settled reading before it is called done. The forecast where the fine tube stops is right on average and scatters by about 0.04 gr on the rig, wider than the band. The waits can be cut to one a throw, not to none.
- **On learning over a session in the PID loop (4):** possible and cheap; the proposal's runs include it.
  - **What it can fix:** it learns a constant offset. The PID loop's over is the flow at its stop times the lag, and the flow at the stop changes from throw to throw, so a learnt offset takes away its average and not its scatter.
  - **What it costs:** on this powder the learning at 15% a throw also walks on the stop's scatter (series E, the play of 12:36).
- **On "run two in parallel":** the agent's view, argued from the runs, was that the accuracy comes from the forecast setting the speed and from a finish that checks the weight once, and that a PID loop on the reading with the forecast as a veto keeps most of its overs. What the PID loop has that is worth keeping, as the agent saw it:
  - its form (gains, a floor and a top) as the way the staged stream is shown and shared, the gains made by the calibration;
  - its going on without waiting, as far as the one settled reading allows.
  - One place where a parallel forecast would pay as it stands: a guard on a hand-tuned PID loop for a user with no calibration. That guard would need the powder's grains a turn, which only a calibration gives, unless it took the flow from the reading's own slope and the lag from the scale's make; not tried.
- **Found on the way, not part of the proposal:** the PID loop's derivative is taken per millisecond (`charge_mode.cpp`, `error_derivative`), so the profiles' Kd of 0.3 and 15 add about 0.01 rev/s: both loops in the field are P-only with limits.
