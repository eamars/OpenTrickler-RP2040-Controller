# A record of talk, 2026-11-22, about 14:10 to 14:29 NZDT. Not a reference

**Read this line first.** The owner put a proposal, in his words "not command or instruction, and I'd like you to think, not record as owner's direction". So this file is what was said while thinking it over, with the simulation runs made to answer it. Nothing here is the owner's direction, a finding of the ADR or a decision, and nothing here is to be quoted as one. The variants tried are in the simulation's code, each marked as from this talk (`legacy_pid.GuardedPid`, `staged.StagedQuickRest`); their figures are in `tools/charge_sim/fit/study_20261122/` (`guarded_pid.txt`, `quick_rest.txt`, `guarded_pid_forecast.txt`), 300 throws of 10 gr each, the simulation kind at the finish as `pid_and_staged_2026-11-22.md` says.

## The owner's proposal, as he wrote it

1. "Waiting for scale to reach steady state is not a good strategy, as some scale has slow reporting interval and longer measurement lag, making the trickling period exponentially longer than A&D, and the current A&D speed with the new algorithm is not acceptable."
2. "Can we combine both, i.e., running prediction in parallel with stream charge, and signal the early stop if the trickling rate is potentially exceeding the charge limit."
3. "If under charge, the controller should continue to top up at minimum speed, with prediction until reaching +stop threshold."
4. "Can we integrate the continuous learning for a session, i.e., no change of powder or quitting the current session etc. into the pid system?"

His verdict, as he gave it: "the new controller is a not a replacement of the PID. Instead I think we should explore the option to run two in parallel, and learn, and signal the early stop if the PID got wrong for a specific trickle?"

## The agent's answers, as given

- **On waiting (1):** half right by these runs.
  - **What ending the wait saves:** ending the rest when the forecast holds nothing more saves 1.5 to 2.5 s and halves the long tail (the 95th percentile 26 to 20 s with AR2209, 73 to 36 s on the slow scale), but costs 6 to 10 points, because powder still lands after the forecast says the air is empty.
  - **Where the time goes on a slow scale:** most of it is not the wait but the approach. For a stop to be accurate, what is in the air at the stop must scatter less than the band; what is in the air is the flow times the scale's lag. So the slower the scale, the slower the flow at the stop must be, and the finish's time grows with the lag: in proportion, not exponentially.
  - **Speed on a slow scale:** a loop that is quick on a slow scale is over (the PID loop: 100%). Speed there has to come from a stop whose powder in the air is better known, not from waiting less.
  - **On the A&D:** the time worth taking is the tail. Series D's throws with no small move took a median 10.7 s; those with moves 12 to 34 s, each move's wait 2 to 4.4 s.
- **On the forecast in parallel with the PID stream (2):**
  - **What it does:** it takes a third of the PID loop's overs away, in less time than the staged controller.
  - **Why it stops there:** the PID loop slows down on the reading, which lags, so the fine tube is still fast when the forecast stops it, and the powder after a fast stop scatters widely. The forecast has to set the speed, not only the stop.
  - **The forecast with the owner's gains:** better with AR2209 and worse with BM2, because those gains were found by hand on the lagging reading. On the forecast the calibrated gain is about 2.2 rev/s a grain against 0.8. A PID loop on the forecast with calibrated gains and a floor set from the scale's lag is what the glide already is.
- **On topping up at the least speed with the forecast (3):** agreed as the way to finish (the glide's creep is that), with two conditions from the runs.
  - **The creep speed:** the least speed must follow the scale and the powder (here 0.029 rev/s, from the band over the lag), not the profile's 0.08, at which a stop's powder scatters by two to three digits.
  - **One settled reading:** a throw at 0.02 gr still needs one settled reading before it is called done. The forecast where the fine tube stops is right on average and scatters by about 0.04 gr on the rig, wider than the band. The waits can be cut to one a throw, not to none.
- **On learning over a session in the PID loop (4):** possible and cheap; the proposal's runs include it.
  - **What it can fix:** it learns a constant offset. The PID loop's over is the flow at its stop times the lag, and the flow at the stop changes from throw to throw, so a learnt offset takes away its average and not its scatter.
  - **What it costs:** on this powder the learning at 15% a throw also walks on the stop's scatter (series E, the play of 12:36).
- **On "run two in parallel":** the agent's view, argued from the runs, was that the accuracy comes from the forecast setting the speed and from a finish that checks the weight once, and that a PID loop on the reading with the forecast as a veto keeps most of its overs. What the PID loop has that is worth keeping, as the agent saw it:
  - its form (gains, a floor and a top) as the way the staged stream is shown and shared, the gains made by the calibration;
  - its going on without waiting, as far as the one settled reading allows.
  - One place where a parallel forecast would pay as it stands: a guard on a hand-tuned PID loop for a user with no calibration. That guard would need the powder's grains a turn, which only a calibration gives, unless it took the flow from the reading's own slope and the lag from the scale's make; not tried.
- **Found on the way, not part of the proposal:** the PID loop's derivative is taken per millisecond (`charge_mode.cpp`, `error_derivative`), so the profiles' Kd of 0.3 and 15 add about 0.01 rev/s: both loops in the field are P-only with limits.

## Later the same afternoon: two modes, tried in the simulation (the owner: "Simulate it first")

The owner asked whether two modes are a fair divide: **fast**, about 10 s a throw, ±0.04 gr, one over in 15; **accurate**, about 20 s, ±0.02 gr, one over in 30. The agent's answer was that a mode can hold two of band, over rate and time, so each would promise its band and its over budget, with time the outcome and a cap. The design tried: one controller, the mode its band, its budget and a time cap, every rule made from those and the calibration:
- the stream's stop aimed from the tail of the rehearsals' own first errors (40 rehearsals, so that a tail can be seen), half the budget given to it;
- fast judges a rest as soon as the forecast holds nothing to show; accurate waits for the settled reading;
- accurate tops up only with a move whose 90% release fits under what is left and the band; fast as today;
- a throw past twice its mode's time ends short (a short throw can be topped up by hand, an over cannot).

**Written before the run.** 300 throws of 10 gr a set, the AR2209 and BM2 records, the measured scale and the slow one:
- **Pass for a mode:** its over share at most its budget (fast 6.7%, accurate 3.3%) on the measured scale, both powders. Time is reported against the targets (10 and 20 s), not judged.
- **Expected:** the simulation's stops scatter less than the rig's, so both pass here more easily than they would on the rig; the rig's own tail is checked apart (the 48 rests of series D and E).

- **Fast: the early verdict hides overs.** Judged as soon as the forecast held nothing more, a rest was read before the last powder had landed. The unit showed good and the cup was over. A hidden over is the worst kind, and the learning was misled by it too: it took the reading for short and moved the stop later (its room from +0.018 to −0.007 gr). The fast mode with the settled verdict is the glide at 0.04 as it is: 8.0% over with AR2209 (one in 12.5, against one in 15) and 0.7% with BM2, both in about 10 s.
- **Accurate: the top-ups make the overs.** 24 of AR2209's 35 overs, and 8 of BM2's 11, came after the first rest, from small moves that released a clump. The smallest move's 90% release is three digits; its tail is beyond the band whatever the move chosen. The stream's stop was also worse than its rehearsals said: the throws' first rests scattered by 0.052 gr, the rehearsals' by 0.045. Rehearsals of a grain from rest are not a throw's last stretch, which gate 3's plan already says.

**The agent's reading:**
- The divide stands as the user's choice.
- The fast mode is near its promise with the settled verdict (one over in 12.5 with AR2209, in the simulation that is kind at the finish).
- The accurate mode's one in 30 does not hold while the top-up is a small move of a clumpy powder. What would have to change is the top-up, not the stream's aim: a top-up whose release has a short tail. That is not tried.

## Evening: two modes or one, learning over a session, and the fine tube turning dry

**The owner (about 15:20):** "So I guess that eliminate the idea of two modes, so we should aim to optimize the fast mode not the accurate mode, as it adds no value or benefits then?" The session's 80 throws judged at ±0.02 gr, whatever mode threw them: accurate 62% within, 38% over, 0% under; fast 57% within, 18% over, 25% under (all at −0.04). The agent agreed for this powder: accurate's top-ups turn shorts into overs about as often as into goods. One caution: the simulation's BM2, whose top-ups are reliable, gains from a ±0.02 finish (95% within 0.02 against 78%). So a BM2 series would settle whether accurate ever adds value.

**The owner:** "Then the next topic: continuous learning: Can the later trickle be minor adjusted based on previous trickle experience?" The agent: the stop is learnt already, and the first rest's median sits at its aim in 6 of the last 8 series; what is left is the stop's scatter (sd 0.04 to 0.06 gr), which no learning from the throw before can predict. Where a session could still learn is the figures that drift: each tube's grains a turn.

**The owner (about 15:25):** "Yes simulate it. And the fine tube is the key. Also, how could we minimise the top up? one observation, not sure if you noticed, the continuous tuning without any powder charge is a bad sign, that it is the early sign of clump powder, and perhaps keep tuning (at least in one direction) isn't a good idea, Do you agree?"

**The observation, tested on the bench's watched nudges** (300 with AR2209 on 11-20, 240 with BM2 on 11-21; a nudge 0.017 turns, its release watched 5 s):

|---|---|---|
| right after a release | 0.32 digits / 4.3% | 0.12 / 1.4% |
| after 1 or 2 dry | 0.76 / 2.8 to 4.2% | 0.47 / 2.4 to 3.2% |

A release after more than 0.05 turns of nothing is about what those turns owed (AR2209 2.7 digits against 2.6; BM2 2.3 against 2.0). So the powder of a dry turn is held at the lip, not lost, and comes out later as one piece. The simulation's tubes do the same (clumps after 4 or more dry: 15% and 7%), so it can judge a mend. The agent agreed with the owner, and named where the controller goes wrong: its forecast drops a parcel once its lag has passed, shown or not, so after a dry top-up it forgets the owed powder and asks for another.

The figure learnt by the end of a session: AR2209 0.773 to 0.666 gr a turn (the rig's throws gave 0.63 to 0.71), BM2 0.550 to 0.413 (25% down, at the limit set: the true figure at the finish is lower still). Throws topped up in the fast mode: AR2209 16 to 18%, BM2 10 to 5%, the owed count changing neither.

**The agent's reading:**
- **Learning the fine tube's figure:** it buys 1.3 to 2.1 s a throw in the fast mode on both powders, with the calibration right or off. It costs a few overs (AR2209 8.0 to 10.7%, 10.0 to 15.3% from a calibration 15% off; BM2 0.7 to 2.3%): the calibration's high figure had been a hidden margin, and the right one lets the glide come in faster. That the learning hits its limit with BM2 says the calibration's figure (from runs at 1 rev/s) is the real fault; measured at the finish's own speeds (gate 3's mend), the session would only trim it.
- **Counting the owed powder:** it takes overs away where there are many top-ups (accurate: AR2209 26 to 18%, BM2 10 to 6%), turning them into unders (2 to 14% with AR2209: the owed powder is sometimes held longer than the wait). In the fast mode, with a top-up in one throw in six, it changes little and lengthens the tail.
- **"Keep tuning in one direction":** agreed, in both places. Don't add top-ups while powder is owed. Learn by a mean over the session, not by a step a throw: a dry throw and the dump after it then cancel. The mean came to the rig's own figure.
- **Minimising the top-ups:** neither idea changes how many there are. A top-up is needed when the stream's stop scatters below the band. What decides that is the aim (each digit nearer the target means fewer shorts and more overs, a trade the over budget sets) and the powder's scatter, which no controller removes. Once one is needed, its cost is its wait, which on the rig is 4.4 s for the smallest move.

**A correction to the run above (15:42), found by the agent while building it for the unit:** the learnt figure's fall (AR2209 0.773 to 0.666, BM2 0.550 to 0.413) and the 1.3 to 2.1 s it bought were an artefact of the variant's window. It took the fine tube's turns a fixed lag (the slowest speed's) before the stretch, while a tube still slowing shows its powder sooner. So it counted turns already shown, and read the figure low. The simulation's fine tube pours the same at every speed (`records/pico2w_ar2209.json`: 0.765 gr a turn). With each parcel's own lag, the learning finds it (0.745) and buys nothing, as it should. The time came from the wrong figure letting the glide come in faster, and so did the overs.

What stands, measured again with the same care (`bench/verify_series.py`, each turn shown only once its own lag at its own speed has passed): the rig's fine tube at the finish pours 0.64 to 0.71 gr a turn (series B 0.711, D 0.654, accurate at 10 gr 0.642, fast at 20 gr 0.664), against the 0.715 thrown with since series D and the PC's 0.797. The old window read the rig's figure 0 to 4% lower than this. So the rig's finish really pours less than its calibration says; the simulation's tube does not, and cannot show the effect by itself. The simulation's test is then a calibration 15% too high, at 5 gr with the fine tube alone (`fit/study_20261122/fine_learning_5gr.txt`, fast, judged at 0.04): learning wins back 0.5 s with AR2209 (19.0 to 18.5 s) and 1.4 s with BM2 (10.4 to 9.0 s), accuracy the same within the noise. A small gain. Whether the rig gives it is the session's question.
