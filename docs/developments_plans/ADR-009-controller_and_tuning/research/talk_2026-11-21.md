## The interim conclusion the agent gave when asked ("Based on the data we have, what is your interim conclusion?")

1. **The tube is a doser of turns.** For both powders and both tubes the powder poured follows the turns made, not the speed. Speed only sets how soon. The calibration's main number is grains a turn, for each tube with each powder.
2. **A move is off by four to five kernels however long it is,** once it is past about half a turn; a move of a few hundredths of a turn is off by one to two digits. So accuracy comes from ending short and adding small moves, not from a better stop.
3. **What a stop leaves, and the delay to the reading, belong to the powder as well as the scale,** and both grow with the speed stopped from: the last speed before a stop is what matters.
4. **The scale shows one digit only after about 2 s** (one time in ten after 3.5 to 4 s); a larger change shows in under a second. A finish that waits for single digits is slow on the A&D and would be far slower on a scale like the owner's G&G.
5. **Turning back is not free and has not been seen to help:** it costs 0.1 to 0.6 of a turn of the forward flow after it, spills kernels with BM2, and back-and-forth was no more even than steady turning.
6. **Published powder data does not give the pour:** two powders of the same bulk density differ by 28% in the fine tube. Each powder is measured on the unit.
7. **The three-part calibration stands, with one change seen so far:** the scale's part is its own; the once-a-turn wave is the tube's; but grains a turn, the stop and the reverse are of tube and powder together (BM2 is 28% under in one tube and 16% under in the other). Which of these a second unit shares is not known.
8. **Not concluded:** any over-rate or time for any controller. The simulation is not yet fitted to these recordings.

The agent's leaning, said with it and not a decision: a hybrid, feeding by counted turns to a point short of the target, then small moves with waits matched to the scale.

## The owner's ideas, put as questions, and the agent's answers

The first, on turning the research to the fine tube, was settled and is in `../README.md`. The other three:

- **A stream in place of bursts** (the owner, 2026-11-21, 03:25, asked as a question): "based on your observation, do you agree that we should try to keep constant stream of trickling, instead of burst. Do you agree?" The agent's answer, not yet answered by the owner and not a design decision: yes as the rule (the tube keeps turning and slows; it does not stop to look), because the scale shows a stream after about 1 s and a single digit after about 2 s, and a scale like the owner's G&G far later. Two limits from the recordings: at the slowest speeds the powder bunches by itself (at 0.05 rev/s a second brought 5 to 8 digits against a mean of 1.3 to 1.7, both powders), so a stream is not even at the end; and the stream's last second is not yet in the reading when the stop is decided, so the stop is by the turns counted ahead of the reading, ending short, with a small move added only when the settled reading is short. Whether that reaches 1 over in 50 is the simulation's to say once it is fitted.
- **Feedback, or feedback with feedforward** (the owner, the same message, as an idea): "do you think feedback controller is sufficient, or should the feedback + feedforward to be used? And perhaps given the lag, should feedforward a negative factor?" The agent's answer, talk and not a decision: both together. Feedback alone works but is capped by the lag (its gain times the lag is bounded, so it is slow or it overshoots, and it holds only near the scale it was set on: `scale_map.py`, and the stored fine Kp of 2 that put 1.3 gr over). Feedforward alone cannot finish: the throw-shaped ramp, the same turns with no controller, ends 4.5 digits (BM2) to 6.6 digits (AR2209) off. The owner's negative term is right in sign: the loop should act on the reading plus the powder already turned out and not yet shown, which is the turns of the last lag times the grains a turn. It is not one factor: it goes with the speed, and the lag itself goes with the flow (a lead, `progress.md` 03:00). Two ideas offered with it: the grains a turn learned inside the throw from the fast part's slope against the turns counted, for a tube that starts low; and the margin learned from throw to throw from where each ended. A caution from this research's own past: `flow_dose`, a model-first controller made before the bench, was 98% over on the measured unit; feedforward is as good as its model and no better.
- **A reverse turn as a retraction** (the owner, 2026-11-21, 03:45, a question): "does the reverser rotation help with retraction?" The agent's answer, talk and not a decision: not at the stop, by what is recorded. It does pull the powder back from the lip (the forward turn after it owes 0.12 to 0.36 of a turn with AR2209 and 0.37 to 0.59 with BM2, more than was turned back), but: what arrives after a stop is already in the air or in the scale's lag, which a reverse cannot take back; the reverse itself throws kernels off (a quarter turn at the stop adds 0.7 to 0.8 digits with AR2209 and 1.6 to 3.0 with BM2; even 0.05 of a turn back adds about 1 digit; the coarse tube's adds about a grain); and the start after it is late by an amount that scatters. Not recorded, so not answered: a reverse of less than 0.05 turn, and whether a retracted tube drips less while standing or when the bench is knocked, which is the one use left for it.
